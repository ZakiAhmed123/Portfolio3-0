Problem statement

Therapists struggled to schedule patients because the system had three outdated schedulers with confusing controls, repeated features, and hard-to-read layouts, making it difficult to use and maintain.

Research Findings

01

Therapists, our core users, are highly educated, which helps with training, but many have used the same legacy systems for years. This requires balancing modern design with familiar workflows to support adoption.

02

Readability, scan-ability and transparency on therapist's availability was lacking in every scheduler, making decision making and initial onboarding for new users challenging.

03

The scheduler had too many redundant legacy and custom fragmented schedulers which were confusing to navigate and learn, training therapists when to use which scheduler was challenging for the business.

04

Therapists move frequently throughout the facility using iPads, so they need a mobile experience that is both readable and easy to tap, with offline capabilities to ensure they can continue working in areas without Wi-Fi access.

05

The legacy schedulers used outdated, custom controls that do not map to modern web components. Each type of scheduling such as patient, therapist, group, queue, and non-therapy was handled in separate modules with no centralized actions, creating a disjointed and inefficient experience.
